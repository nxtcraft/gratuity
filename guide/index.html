What is Gratuity?

Gratuity is a lump sum payment made by an employer to an employee as a token of appreciation for long service. It is mandatory under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 for companies with 10 or more employees. Once a company comes under the Act, it continues to apply even if the headcount drops below 10.

Gratuity is paid when an employee leaves a company after completing 5 or more years of service — whether due to retirement, resignation, termination (not for misconduct), or retrenchment. It is also paid to the nominee or legal heir in case of death or permanent disability, regardless of service duration.

Covered under the Act: Any establishment (factory, mine, oilfield, plantation, port, railway company, shop or any other establishment) employing 10 or more persons on any day in the preceding 12 months.

Gratuity Formula — The 15/26 Rule Explained

Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 — Formula
Gratuity = (Last Basic + DA) × 15/26 × Completed Years of Service

Where: 26 = working days per month | 15 = days' salary per year of service

The 26 in the denominator represents the number of working days in a month (4 Sundays are excluded from 30 days). The 15 represents 15 days' pay per year of service — the gratuity entitlement.

Worked Example 1: Standard Gratuity

Employee resigns after 8 years 3 months — Basic+DA = ₹45,000/month Completed years = 8 (3 months < 6, so it doesn't round up)
Gratuity = ₹45,000 × 15/26 × 8 = ₹45,000 × 0.5769 × 8 = ₹2,07,692

Worked Example 2: Rounding Up

Employee retires after 25 years 7 months — Basic+DA = ₹80,000/month Completed years = 26 (7 months ≥ 6, rounds up to 26)
Gratuity = ₹80,000 × 15/26 × 26 = ₹80,000 × 15 = ₹12,00,000

⚠️ The maximum gratuity payable under the Act is ₹20,00,000. Even if the calculated amount exceeds ₹20 lakh, the statutory limit caps it at ₹20 lakh. Employers can voluntarily pay more ("ex-gratia"), but the tax exemption only covers ₹20 lakh.

Eligibility — 5-Year Rule & Exceptions

The standard rule: 5 years of continuous service at the same employer. "Continuous" includes approved leaves, layoffs, and lock-outs — only intentional absence without approval breaks continuity.

When Gratuity is Paid:

  • Resignation: Minimum 5 years required
  • Retirement: Minimum 5 years required (usually far exceeded)
  • Retrenchment: Minimum 5 years required
  • Death: No minimum — any duration qualifies; paid to nominee
  • Permanent disability: No minimum — gratuity paid regardless of service length
  • Termination for misconduct: Gratuity can be forfeited (fully or partly) under specific conditions
5-year calculation nuance: Some courts have interpreted "5 years of continuous service" as 4 years and 240 days (since the standard working year is 240 days for employees working 6 days/week). This interpretation is debated — practically, most companies and legal advisors use the calendar 5-year rule.

Who Receives Gratuity if the Employee Passes Away?

The gratuity is paid to the nominee registered with the employer. If no nominee is registered, it is paid to the legal heir. The nominee can be changed at any time by submitting Form F to the employer. Death gratuity from the government sector is fully tax-free; from private sector it follows the same ₹20 lakh exemption rule.

Tax Calculation — Private Sector Employee Gratuity received = ₹22,00,000 | Exemption = ₹20,00,000 | Taxable = ₹2,00,000
If employee is in 30% slab: Tax on ₹2,00,000 = ₹60,000 + surcharge (if applicable)

Gratuity from more than one employer during your lifetime is aggregated for the ₹20 lakh exemption. If you received ₹12 lakh from a previous employer (exempt) and later receive ₹15 lakh from another, only ₹8 lakh is exempt from the second (total ₹20 lakh cap across lifetime).

Leave Encashment — Section 10(10AA)

Leave encashment is the payment you receive for unused earned/privilege leave at the time of separation. It is separate from gratuity and has its own tax rules.

SituationTax Treatment

At retirement/resignation — Private employeeExempt up to minimum of (a), (b), (c) below

Section 10(10AA) Exemption for Private Employees

The exemption is the least of:

  1. Actual leave encashment received
  2. ₹25,00,000 (₹25 lakh — raised from ₹3 lakh in Budget 2023)
  3. Average salary of last 10 months × number of months of earned leave (maximum 30 days per year of service)
Example: 15 years of service, 20 days leave accumulated, last drawn salary ₹60,000/month (a) Actual = 20 × 2,000 = ₹40,000 (₹60,000 ÷ 30 × 20)
(b) ₹25 lakh limit
(c) 10 months avg salary × months leave = ₹60,000 × (20/30) = ₹40,000
Exemption = Least of above = ₹40,000 (fully exempt in this case)

Full & Final Settlement — What You Receive When You Leave

Full & Final (F&F) settlement is the complete settlement of all dues when an employee exits an organization. It typically includes multiple components:

ComponentIncluded IfTaxable?
Gratuity5+ years serviceExempt up to ₹20L
Leave encashmentUnused earned leave balanceExempt up to ₹25L at resignation/retirement
Pending salaryAlways — for days worked in final monthFully taxable
Notice period salaryIf company waives notice period (pays in lieu)Fully taxable
Performance bonus / variable payIf accrued and dueFully taxable
Expense reimbursementsPending claimsNot taxable (reimbursement)

F&F Timeline

  • Gratuity must be paid within 30 days of the date it becomes due
  • If delayed beyond 30 days, the employer must pay simple interest at the rate prescribed (currently 10% p.a.)
  • Notice period recovery: if you leave without serving notice, the company can deduct notice period pay from the F&F
⚠️ If your employer refuses to pay gratuity after 5 years, you can file an application with the Controlling Authority (Labour Commissioner) in your area under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972. The employer is liable to pay the amount with interest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is gratuity part of CTC or is it paid extra?

Many employers include the "gratuity provision" in the CTC (Cost to Company) as an accrual (typically 4.81% of basic — which equals Basic × 15/26 ÷ 12). However, gratuity is NOT deducted from your salary — it is paid entirely by the employer when you leave. If your offer letter shows CTC including gratuity, it means the company is accounting for the future liability; you do not forfeit any take-home salary for gratuity.

Does gratuity apply to contract workers and consultants?

Gratuity under the Payment of Gratuity Act applies to "employees" — defined as persons employed on wages in an establishment. Contract workers deployed through a staffing agency are typically employees of the contractor/staffing firm, not the principal employer. Consultants and freelancers (independent contractors) are not covered. If a contract worker has worked at the same establishment for 5+ years as an employee (even through rotation of contracts), they may be entitled to gratuity — this is an ongoing legal grey area.

Can my employer deduct from gratuity if I resign mid-month?

No. Under the Payment of Gratuity Act, the gratuity amount is calculated on the last drawn basic + DA and years of service. The employer cannot deduct from gratuity for mid-month resignation. However, they can recover notice period pay (from pending salary, not from gratuity), outstanding loans, or any other dues separately — but not from the statutory gratuity amount.

What happens to gratuity if a company shuts down?

If a company closes, all employees (even those with less than 5 years' service) are entitled to gratuity, calculated as 7 days' salary per year of service (instead of the usual 15 days). This is a special provision for closure cases under Section 4(5) of the Act. Gratuity is treated as a preferential debt — meaning employees are paid before most other creditors during liquidation proceedings.
